Hosted service privacy notice

Scope and responsible person

Breadmaker’s hosted service is operated by Mateo Saldivar Cueto Felgueroso. Contact privacy@breadmaker.studio for privacy questions and requests.

This notice covers information processed for hosted Breadmaker accounts, hosted projects and the operational counts described below. It is not a notice for unrelated websites or services. Running the open-source Editor locally is different: ordinary local writing does not require a Breadmaker account, and local project files stay on your computer unless you use a network feature or send them elsewhere.

Information and purposes

Account and access information includes your email address, account and authentication-provider identifiers, verified-email status, admission status and associated profile information. Google Identity Platform/Firebase Authentication handles sign-in. Email/password and Google sign-in are enabled. A provider account can be created before admission to the hosted alpha. We use this information to authenticate you and apply account and project permissions.

Hosted project information includes the writing, notes, planning material, tasks, reviews, files, images, revisions and settings you save, and the identifiers needed to provide these features. We process it to save, retrieve, synchronize and display your projects, provide features you request and support recovery functions. Hosted processing is not end-to-end encrypted: Breadmaker’s servers can process readable content. Cloud encryption does not make the service zero knowledge.

Connection, security and service records are processed to deliver requests, diagnose failures and protect the service. A request can involve connection information such as your IP address and request timing. Infrastructure logs and application diagnostics are separate from the operational counts below.

If you contact us, we process your contact information and the information needed to understand and answer your request. Please do not send passwords, authentication codes or unnecessary manuscript excerpts. We do not ask for sensitive personal information for sign-in or operational counts. Writing you choose to store can itself contain personal or sensitive information about you or others; it remains hosted content and is processed to provide the features you request, not as input to these counts.

Operational counts

When the operational-count feature is enabled, Breadmaker calculates, for service capacity and cost management, the number of application profiles and project records explicitly marked active. These calculations use account and project status metadata. They do not enumerate manuscript content or return names, email addresses or individual source records to Admin.

When the owner opens Admin, it can display cached results while requesting an automatic refresh. Automatic refreshes have a 15-minute cooldown. The owner can also request a manual refresh without waiting for that interval; manual refreshes still share the limit of 120 refresh attempts per month and controls designed to prevent overlapping refreshes. There is no timer-based polling or scheduler for these counts. If a refresh cannot complete, an earlier result may remain visible with its original observation time; results older than 15 minutes are not presented as current. Results above the supported limit of 100,000 records are unavailable rather than estimated.

The counts exclude records without an explicit active status. They do not show people currently online, lifetime registrations or individual writing activity. This feature does not enable optional local Editor usage reporting and does not collect word, character, canvas or presence totals. It is for internal service management, not advertising, targeting or decisions about individual users.

Optional Targets and narration

Targets is off by default. If you enable it, it records the goals you select, net word or character progress, relevant foreground or active-time intervals for time goals, goal periods and achievement history. Hosted Targets data synchronize to account-scoped project storage; they are not anonymous statistics. Browser localStorage and IndexedDB can retain pending updates and synchronization identifiers. Turning Targets off stops new tracking but preserves saved goals and history. Clearing browser storage does not delete hosted copies. You may request help concerning retained Targets data through the privacy contact above.

Speech generation runs on your device where narration is available. Hosted pronunciation processing for supported language features can send the relevant text or word lists to Breadmaker’s backend and its private pronunciation helper. Saved recordings, related source excerpts, voice choices and timing information are hosted data. Device-side speech generation does not mean that all narration-related processing stays on your device.

Providers, location and access

The hosted service uses Google Cloud services, including Identity Platform/Firebase Authentication, Firestore, Cloud Storage and application infrastructure. The main application, database and project-storage configuration uses the us-central1 region in the United States. This does not mean that authentication, network delivery, logs or every provider operation happens only there. Google processes information to supply the infrastructure and authentication services used by Breadmaker. Google’s own processing information is available in its privacy policy.

Human support access to your content requires your specific approval for the relevant problem and content. This is an access policy, not a claim that technical measures make all operator access impossible. It does not change the automated server processing needed to provide hosted features. Any legally binding demand must be assessed individually under applicable law; it does not establish routine support access.

If you grant another person access through project or review permissions, the selected information is available to those authorized people according to the feature’s permissions. Optional connections you initiate can send information to the destination you choose. For example, local Editor Google Drive backups send backup packages to the Google account you authorize. Copies in those destinations are separate from hosted copies.

Cookies and device storage

The hosted service uses an authentication cookie to recognize a signed-in account and a short-lived security cookie for authentication requests. Its authentication client is configured to keep sign-in state in memory. Browser storage can retain interface preferences, including language, theme and writing-display settings, performance calibration, feature caches, pending Targets updates and recovery drafts. Recovery drafts in IndexedDB can contain manuscript text, chapter titles, account/project identifiers, revision information and save times. These private drafts are distinct from cached public narration-model files. Drafts may remain between sessions until handled by the relevant save, recovery or discard flow, or cleared through browser controls. Clearing browser storage may sign you out, reset preferences or discard unsynchronized writing; it does not erase data already stored on the server.

Retention and current deletion limits

Account and project information is retained to provide the hosted service and its history and recovery features. Closing an account, removing an item from the workspace and erasing all copies are different actions. Account closure alone does not erase all hosted data. Some physical-erasure and restoration-suppression processes are not complete. This technical limitation does not remove your legal rights or justify ignoring a request. We assess requests individually, explain what can be done and identify any specific lawful reason for retaining information.

The operational-count feature keeps one current aggregate/control record, including controls that limit refresh requests. It keeps no separate trend history. The current record remains until replaced or retired. Marking a cached result as old or hiding it is not deletion. Replaced database versions may remain recoverable through the existing seven-day point-in-time recovery system. This feature is excluded from ordinary project exports and recovery archives. Separate backups or exports that would retain its replaced records beyond this period must not be introduced without revisiting this retention statement.

Firestore point-in-time recovery can retain earlier document versions within a seven-day window. Project-content and asset buckets have object versioning and 30-day soft deletion. Recovery archives also have a 30-day minimum retention setting; the inspected settings do not schedule age-based deletion of those archives. A minimum retention period prevents early deletion; it does not promise later deletion. Separate older versions or archives can remain beyond a soft-deletion window.

Narration object storage is configured without object versioning or soft deletion, but related records and copies require separate handling. Automatic narration cleanup is disabled in the inspected configuration. Closing an account does not establish that audio or all related records were erased.

The standard Google Cloud Logging _Default bucket has 30-day retention; the _Required bucket has locked 400-day retention. These are log-bucket periods, not a promise that every provider record follows those periods. Copies you download or store in destinations you control remain under your control.

Your rights and requests

Mateo Saldivar Cueto Felgueroso handles requests at privacy@breadmaker.studio. You may request access, correction, cancellation or objection concerning your personal information, and withdraw consent where processing relies on it. Other applicable laws may provide additional rights, including erasure, restriction or portability.

Include your name, a way to reply, the action requested and enough information to locate the relevant records. For correction, describe the correction and supporting information. A representative may act for you. We may ask for proportionate information to verify identity or authority before disclosing or changing private records. Do not send identity documents or sensitive material unless specifically needed and a suitable submission method has been agreed.

We will respond using the verified contact or another agreed appropriate method. We will not send manuscript content or other people’s data merely because an email requests it. We will explain any refusal or partial fulfillment and the relevant reason. Requests and necessary verification records are kept only as needed to handle the request and demonstrate compliance with applicable obligations; unnecessary sensitive material should not be retained.

Under Mexican private-sector data-protection law, the decision on an ARCO request is normally due within 20 business days of receipt. If granted, action is due within 15 business days after the response is communicated. Each period may be extended once by an equal period where justified. Exercising ARCO rights is free, subject only to charges permitted by law. Other applicable laws may provide different rights or deadlines.

If you disagree with an ARCO response, or receive no response within the applicable period, you may seek protection from Mexico’s Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno. Articles 40–41 of the LFPDPPP describe the procedure, including the usual 15-business-day filing period after a response. Rights can have lawful limits, including the rights of others and specific legal-retention requirements.

Legal grounds and choices

We process account and hosted-project information to provide the services you request and meet the obligations arising from that relationship, where applicable law permits that ground. The two operational counts are limited to managing service capacity and costs. Where applicable law permits, these minimized operational counts support our legitimate interest in keeping the hosted service reliable and within capacity and cost limits. Where a legal-relationship exception applies, processing is limited to what is required to fulfill that relationship. These grounds do not authorize unrelated analytics.

Where processing relies on consent, you may withdraw it through the privacy contact. Optional Targets can also be turned off in the feature controls, although existing records remain subject to the retention and request process above. Where applicable law gives a right to object, we will assess it and stop or restrict processing when required.

Age and notice changes

The hosted service requires the country-specific minimum age in Breadmaker’s age requirements, based on Google’s minimum ages for managing an independent account. The minimum is 13 in Mexico and countries without a higher listed age. Breadmaker currently offers no supervised or parent-managed hosted account for younger users. This eligibility rule is not a claim that every user’s age has been verified.

Changes will be posted at the hosted privacy-notice link with a publication date and version. Before starting a materially different purpose, we will provide the notice and any consent or other choice required by applicable law. You can contact privacy@breadmaker.studio for the current notice or questions about a change.
